LESSONS WRITTEN IN SCARS

They're Watching

Our children will hear what we say, but they'll learn from how we live.

As fathers, we give a lot of advice.

We tell our children to tell the truth.

Treat people with respect.

Keep their word.

Own their mistakes.

Do the right thing even when nobody is watching.

And there’s nothing wrong with that. Part of being a father is helping shape our children into the people we hope they’ll become.

But there’s another part that can be a little harder.

Living by the same words we give them.

I say that because I’ve had to look at myself and realize there have been times when my words and my actions didn’t match.

It’s easy to tell our children what the right thing is.

Living it is different.

We can tell them to control their anger, but what happens when they watch us lose ours?

We can teach them about honesty, but what happens when they catch us being dishonest?

We can tell them to own their mistakes, but what happens when we’re too proud to apologize for ours?

Eventually, our children become old enough to notice the difference.

They’re watching.

And I think that’s where respect comes in.

We can’t demand respect from our children simply because we’re their fathers.

Some of it has to be earned.

They have to see that the man giving them advice is willing to live by it himself.

That doesn’t mean being perfect.

We’re going to screw up.

We’re going to have moments when we don’t live up to the things we’ve taught them.

I know I have.

But even those moments give us an opportunity to practice what we preach.

Own it.

Apologize.

Make it right.

Do better next time.

Because accountability isn’t something I only want my children to learn.

It’s something I want them to see.

The older my daughters get, the more I realize my words will only carry so much weight.

How I live will carry more.

If I want them to stand for what’s right, they need to see me stand for what’s right.

If I want them to admit when they’re wrong, they need to see me admit when I’m wrong.

If I want them to become people of integrity, then integrity has to start with me.

I’m not asking my children to follow a perfect man.

They won’t find one here.

But I do want the things I teach them to look familiar.

Because they’ve already seen me trying to live them.
