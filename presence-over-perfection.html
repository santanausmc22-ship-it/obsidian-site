LESSONS WRITTEN IN SCARS

Presence Over Perfection

Fatherhood isn't about getting everything right. It's about refusing to stop showing up.

I still remember the moment my wife told me we were going to have a baby.

For a second, everything stood still.

I wish I could tell you my first emotion was excitement—that I immediately pictured teaching my child to ride a bike or cheering from the stands at every game and recital.

It wasn’t.

It was fear.

Not because I didn’t want to be a father, but because I had no idea what being a father actually looked like.

I grew up wondering what it would have been like to have a father who was truly present. Someone who taught me the little things most people take for granted. How to change a tire. How to throw a baseball. How to become a man.

Looking back, I realize I wasn’t really grieving the lessons.

I was grieving the presence.

And suddenly, life was asking me to become the very man I’d spent years wishing I had.

I remember leaving the hospital with my child for the first time, looking around almost expecting someone to stop us.

“You’re really trusting us with this?”

We had no manual. No experience. Just two scared parents trying to figure it out.

That drive home changed everything.

Over the years, I’ve realized that fatherhood was never about perfection.

It’s about presence.

Showing up when you’re exhausted.

Showing up after you’ve lost your temper.

Showing up when shame whispers that your children deserve someone better.

Showing up when you don’t have all the answers but refuse to stop trying.

Because every day you choose to come home, to apologize, to learn, and to love, you’re teaching your children something perfection never could.

I’m still learning.

There are days I feel like I’m getting it right, and there are days I lie awake wondering if I’m giving my children everything they deserve. I don’t think that feeling ever completely disappears.

Maybe that’s because the fathers who worry the most are often the ones who care the deepest.

This isn’t written by a parenting expert.

It’s written by a father who’s still becoming the man his children deserve.

If you’re reading this and you’ve ever felt like you’re falling short, I want you to know something.

You’re not alone.

This isn’t a place where fathers come pretending they’ve figured everything out.

It’s a place where we can be honest.

A place where we can admit we’re tired.

A place where we can talk about the weight we carry, the mistakes we’ve made, and the fear that somehow we aren’t enough.

It’s a place for the fathers trying to break generational cycles.

For the dads carrying wounds from their own childhood.

For the men who keep questioning themselves because they care so deeply about getting it right.

If that’s you, welcome.

Because your children won’t remember every lesson you tried to teach.

They won’t remember every word you said.

They may not even remember every mistake you made.

But I hope they remember one thing.

Dad was there.
