LESSONS WRITTEN IN SCARS

Outnumbered

I thought I might raise a son. Instead, three daughters helped raise a different version of me.

Before I became a father, I always imagined having a boy.

I pictured throwing a football around.

Teaching him things.

Doing all the things I thought fathers and sons were supposed to do.

Then God gave me three girls.

And apparently He wanted to make absolutely sure I got the message.

Three daughters.

Sixteen chickens. All hens.

Two dogs. Both girls.

An axolotl. Girl.

A bearded dragon. Also a girl.

I am completely outnumbered.

And I wouldn’t change it.

I don’t know what it’s like to raise a boy.

I don’t know if I would’ve been tougher with a son. Rougher. Maybe I would’ve approached fatherhood differently.

I’ll never know.

What I do know is what raising three girls has done to me.

My daughters are completely different from one another.

They love differently.

They communicate differently.

They get angry differently.

They need me differently.

And yes, there are a lot of emotions in my house.

A lot.

There are arguments over things I don’t understand.

Tears over things that seemed completely fine five minutes earlier.

Doors close.

Then somehow everyone is laughing again.

I’ve had to learn patience in ways I never expected.

But I’ve also learned something else.

Strength and tenderness can exist in the same man.

My daughters need me to protect them, but they also need me to listen.

They need boundaries, but they also need compassion.

They need correction, but they need to know that correction doesn’t change how deeply they’re loved.

And they need to hear that they’re loved.

Often.

The other day, we were at a birthday party.

Someone asked a group of kids which one of their friends was the nurturing one. The one who naturally cared for everyone else.

Everyone pointed to my daughter.

My wife and I looked at each other.

It was one of those small moments nobody else in the room would probably think twice about.

But I did.

Because I saw something in my daughter that I hope we’re putting into all three of them.

Compassion.

Love.

The ability to care about another person.

They’re all different, and they’ll show those things differently, but I see it in each of them.

And maybe we’re doing something right.

I tell my girls all the time that women are powerful.

I’ve seen firsthand what having a strong woman beside a man can do.

So I’m not raising my daughters simply hoping that one day they’ll become someone’s wife.

I’m raising them to know who they are before anyone else gets a say in it.

I want them to know there are no boundaries on what they’re capable of.

Don’t put yourself in a box because the world handed you one.

Break through it.

Become whatever God has called you to become.

And if one day they choose to build a life with someone, I hope they bring that same strength, compassion, love, and confidence with them.

I started fatherhood thinking about some imaginary little boy I’d throw a football with.

Instead, I got little hands wrapped around mine.

Hugs I didn’t know I needed.

Three completely different personalities that challenge me in three completely different ways.

And more emotions than one man should probably be legally required to handle.

I’m still learning.

Still losing my patience sometimes.

Still getting things wrong.

Still trying to understand how something that seems so small to me can feel so big to them.

But being their father has softened parts of me I didn’t know needed softening.

It’s made me listen differently.

Love differently.

It’s made me different.

I don’t know what kind of father I would’ve been to a boy.

But I know the father I’m trying to be for my girls.

And when I look around at this loud, emotional, chaotic, completely female household of mine…

I don’t feel like I’m missing anything.

I feel blessed to be outnumbered.
