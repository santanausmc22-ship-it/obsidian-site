LESSONS WRITTEN IN SCARS

The Man Behind the Father

Sometimes we get so focused on being what everyone needs that we forget there's still a person underneath it all.

How can I be a good father when I never witnessed one?

How can I be a good husband when I never witnessed a good relationship between my parents?

We grow from what we witness.

We learn how to love from the love we were shown. We learn how to handle anger, disagreement, affection, and pain from the people who came before us.

Then one day we grow up and realize some of those things have to be unlearned.

I was sitting in church today with my oldest child beside me, holding onto my arm.

For a moment, I found myself thinking about the last year of my life.

I’ve changed.

I’ve grown.

I’ve become a better father.

I’m trying to become a better husband.

And I’m still fighting parts of the man I used to be.

Today, my child was actually listening to the sermon.

That might sound small, but to me it felt like a win.

I sat there with her holding my arm and thought maybe some of this is working.

Maybe all the conversations, the prayers, the mistakes, the apologies, the showing up and trying again are actually leading somewhere.

Then you leave church.

And life is still waiting outside.

The problems are still there.

The things you’re trying to repair are still there.

And somehow you can know you’ve traveled miles and still feel like you haven’t moved at all.

That’s when a question hit me today that I don’t think I’ve allowed myself to ask very often.

What do I need?

Not what do my children need?

Not what does my family need?

Not what problem needs to be fixed next?

What do I need?

And almost immediately, I felt guilty for asking.

That’s hard to admit.

I spend so much of my life trying to be the solution.

Protect.

Provide.

Fix.

Carry.

Stand firm.

Keep everyone moving forward.

I think a lot of fathers know that feeling.

We become so used to being needed that sometimes we forget we’re allowed to need something too.

And maybe that’s where I’m at right now.

Not with an answer.

Just a question.

Is it okay to want more?

More connection.

More peace.

More understanding.

More moments where I’m not needed because something is wrong, but simply wanted because I’m me.

I don’t think wanting those things makes me less of a father.

I don’t think it makes me less of a husband.

But I’d be lying if I said believing that comes naturally to me.

There’s still a part of me that believes my value comes from what I can carry for everyone else.

That my job is to be the solution, not part of the equation.

Maybe that’s another thing I have to unlearn.

Because underneath the responsibility, underneath the title of husband, underneath being Dad, there’s still a man there.

A man who gets tired.

A man who needs connection.

A man who sometimes needs to know he’s wanted and not just needed.

Maybe taking care of that man isn’t taking something away from my family.

Maybe it’s part of becoming the father and husband I’m trying so hard to be.

I started today wondering how a man becomes a good father when he never witnessed one.

I still don’t know if I have the answer.

Maybe we don’t become better men by pretending the past didn’t shape us.

Maybe we become better by recognizing where it did—and deciding what we’re going to do differently.

I know what kind of father I want to be.

I know what kind of husband I’m trying to become.

I’m just starting to realize that somewhere in between those two things, I also have to figure out how to take care of the man behind them.

I don’t have an answer for that yet.

Today, I’m just finally allowing myself to ask the question.
